# Pasadena ↔ Eagle Rock ↔ Glendale Corridor Decision Brief

**Relay Rider product prototype — partner-facing demonstration brief**

Relay Rider is an employer- and institution-funded commuter coordination and Transportation Demand Management platform. It converts commute-pattern, parking-pressure, planned-route, proposed-contribution, EV/hybrid and Access Point signals into corridor intelligence, governed commuter-option previews, administrative tasks and partner-ready reports.

This brief is a **demonstration framework**. It does not represent live transportation service, a confirmed route, a fare transaction, a guaranteed outcome, or validated corridor demand.

## Decision question

**Where should an institution focus its next commuter-program action along the Pasadena ↔ Eagle Rock ↔ Glendale corridor, and what evidence should be collected before committing resources?**

## Operating spine

**Signal → Record → Score → Preview → Task → Review → Dashboard → Report → Partner Action**

## Demonstration finding

A synthetic morning commuter cluster can be shown moving from Eagle Rock / Glendale-side approximate zones toward Pasadena institutional destinations. The prototype map can place that demand in context with transit anchors, institution anchors and candidate Access Points.

The purpose is not to claim that a route should operate. The purpose is to show how an administrator can diagnose constraints such as:

- insufficient planned-route capacity
- weak time-window compatibility
- high estimated detour
- unresolved Access Point suitability
- cohort/program eligibility constraints
- proposed-contribution gaps
- incomplete commuter data

| Field | Demonstration output |

## Parking-pressure use case

Relay Rider should help an institution separate at least four forms of pressure:

1. **Availability pressure** — reported difficulty finding a space or capacity constraints.
2. **Search-time pressure** — time spent locating parking.
3. **Cost pressure** — parking cost burden or incentive sensitivity.
4. **Overflow/spillover pressure** — demand shifting into nearby streets/lots or other facilities.

A high score is not a promise that Relay Rider will reduce parking. It should trigger a governed recommendation such as:

- collect additional commute data
- target a specific employee/student cohort
- test an institution-sponsored incentive scenario
- review transit/multimodal alternatives
- review Access Point candidates
- recruit planned-route participants
- continue monitoring because evidence is insufficient

## EV / charging-interest use case

With opt-in commuter data, Relay Rider can summarize:

- EV/hybrid participation signal
- charging-interest signal
- approximate arrival window
- expected dwell window
- current home/work charging access when provided

These outputs are directional planning inputs only. A charger deployment decision still requires site, electrical, utility, cost and facilities analysis.

## Access Point use case

Candidate Access Points should be evaluated using:

- corridor demand proximity
- planned-route overlap
- estimated detour impact
- transit adjacency
- public visibility and general suitability observations
- accessibility observations
- property/site rules
- institution/site approval status

Use the status sequence **Candidate → Under Review → Designated → Rejected**. Relay Rider does not guarantee safety or suitability.

## Guardrails

Relay Rider is not ride-hailing, taxi, shuttle, live dispatch, public booking, instant pickup, or guaranteed transportation. A proposed contribution is a willingness-to-contribute / participation signal, not a confirmed fare or transportation purchase. All commuter options remain subject to program rules and administrative review.
